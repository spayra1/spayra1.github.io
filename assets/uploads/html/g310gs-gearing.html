How this works, and what to trust

Gear ratios. Primary 3.083 and gearbox 3.000 / 2.063 / 1.588 / 1.286 / 1.095 / 0.955 come from owner-posted manual data. One forum post lists the same numbers shifted by one position, so run the tach check: with these defaults, stock gearing gives about 100 rpm per true mph in 6th, which matches owner reports of ~7,200 rpm at 70.

Speedometer. Dash speed reads high on most bikes. Your report of 7,500 rpm around 80 indicated fits a speedometer reading 5–7% high. The G 310 GS takes road speed from a wheel sensor, so a sprocket change should not alter the speedometer, but confirm against GPS after the swap.

Wheel torque. Swapping one sprocket scales rear-wheel torque in every gear by exactly the final-drive change. 16→17 front is −5.9%; 17/42 is only −1.2% versus stock, so it mostly cancels the point of the swap.

Acceleration model. Thrust minus aerodynamic drag, rolling resistance and grade, divided by mass plus the rotating inertia of wheels and engine (the engine term is large in low gears, which softens the loss from taller gearing). Shift time is ignored, so best-gear times are slightly optimistic for everyone equally.

Hill start. Modeled as a clutch-slip launch holding the launch rpm until first gear catches up. Clutch-out speed is how fast the bike is moving when the clutch fully engages; a higher number means more slipping on a steep start.

Torque curve. The default is an estimate, not a dyno sheet. Absolute times will shift with a real curve; the differences between setups hold up much better because they all share the same engine.
